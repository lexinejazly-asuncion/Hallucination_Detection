Does the source actually say that?

Pipeline

How a claim gets checked

The order matters. Splitting before any preprocessing keeps the held-out set honest, and features are audited before they're trusted.

01

Split first

Stratified on label and source dataset together, so no source dominates training and a sealed test set never gets touched during development.

02

Measure overlap

Word and bigram coverage between claim and document, plus whether every number in the claim actually appears in the source.

03

Add meaning

Sentence embeddings catch paraphrases that share no exact wording with the source, on top of the lexical features.

04

Classify and score

A calibrated classifier returns a grounded/hallucinated call with a probability, not just a binary label.

Known limitation

Overlap-based features can't catch a claim that reuses the document's own words and rearranges them into something the document never said. Every word checks out; the meaning doesn't. Tab 03 above shows this happening live — it's a gap in how meaning is represented, not a wall, and a cross-encoder that reads claim and document together is the planned fix.
